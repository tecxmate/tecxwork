TECXWORK User Manual

TECXWORK is a hiring platform for Vietnamese and Indonesian applicants seeking white-collar work in Taiwan. Applicants find jobs at Taiwanese companies and book a real interview in a few minutes. Employers post their open roles and choose who to meet. And the recruitment agency working between them tracks every candidate from first application through to first day at work.

This manual covers all of it — every screen, every button, and the job each one does — for the four kinds of people who use the platform. It is organised around the work they each do rather than around the software, so you can read one part start to finish and come away knowing how to do the whole task.

New here? Read How to read this manual and How a placement happens first — about ten minutes, and everything after it will make sense.
Already know the platform? Go straight to Applicant, Employer, Agency or Admin.

What the platform is

TECXWORK moves Vietnamese and Indonesian applicants into white-collar jobs at Taiwanese employers. It is operated by a recruitment agency — in this build, 揚運國際集團 Yang Luck — which sits between the two sides and gets paid when someone is actually hired.

That agency model is the single most important thing to understand, because it shapes every screen. The agency does not hire for itself. It recruits on behalf of 25 client companies, so almost everything in the product is scoped per client rather than global.

The four parties

PartyWho they areWhat they want
The applicant Vietnamese or Indonesian, usually in their final year or master's at a Taiwanese university. A job in Taiwan that sponsors a work permit.
The client company Mid-size Taiwanese construction, manufacturing, MEP and hospitality firms — Giant, HIWIN, Largan, Lee Ming Construction. To fill entry-level and trainee roles they struggle to staff locally.
The agency Yang Luck 揚運 — the operator, with six group subsidiaries and 19 external clients. To place candidates and get paid on placement.
The platform admin Whoever runs the event or the platform itself. That employers are legitimate and job posts are accurate.

Three products, one codebase

1 · The marketplace

Public and applicant-facing. A company directory, a jobs board, job detail pages, and an application that ends in a confirmed interview time.

  • Applicants apply to a position, not a company
  • Every application reserves a specific time slot
  • The employer accepts, waitlists, reschedules, or rejects
2 · The ATS

Yang Luck is a staffing agency: it does not hire for itself, it places people into client companies. So the pipeline is scoped per client.

  • 25 client accounts, each with its own board
  • Five stages from Applied to Hired
  • Document-expiry compliance for migrant workers
3 · The live job fair

An event-operations system for a single day where hundreds of interviews are scheduled against real, finite capacity — and matching runs in both directions.

  • Applicants book recruiters and recruiters book applicants
  • Capacity is real: interviewers per company, slots per hour
  • Seats are claimed atomically, so nothing double-books

The three share one login, one candidate record and one set of components, which is why they read as a single product. But they answer different questions — who is hiring, where is each candidate up to, and who is meeting whom, when — and the third is the one most easily mistaken for a calendar. It isn't: see How a live job fair runs.

This also explains most of the navigation. A client-company recruiter sees only their own applicants and interviews. The agency recruiter sees the pipeline, clients, compliance and reports across all 25 companies. Both are the recruiter role — the navigation differs by what the account owns.

How a live job fair runs

The third product, and the one that looks deceptively like a calendar. On event day the constraint is physical: a company has a fixed number of interviewers, each interview takes a fixed time, and two applicants cannot sit in the same chair. Everything below exists to keep that constraint true while hundreds of people book at once.

Why it needs to be its own product

A job board can afford to be eventually consistent — a stale listing is a minor annoyance. A job fair cannot: at 14:00 a real person either has a chair or is standing in a corridor.

  • Capacity is finite and known in advance
  • Demand is bursty — everyone books in the same few hours
  • A double-booking is visible to two strangers at once
What the organiser controls

All of it from Platform settings, without touching code.

  • Event date, opening and closing time
  • Slot length and the buffer between interviews
  • Whether applicants may cancel their own bookings
  • Per company: how many interviewers are on site
Where to see this working. The organiser's live view of every booking is 4.1 Every booking; the company's own run sheet is 2.6 Interview day; the applicant's side of the same transaction is 1.10 Picking a time.

Vocabulary

Terms used throughout the manual and inside the product. Several are staffing-industry or Taiwanese-regulatory words that mean something specific here.

Applicant 求職者
Someone looking for work on the platform. This is the account role in the system (applicant) and the word this manual uses throughout. A few screens still say Student — the sign-up chooser's I'm a Student button and the STUDENT column on the admin bookings table — because most applicants are final-year university students. Same person either way.
Position 職缺
A single job opening at one company. The unit applicants apply to — never a company on its own.
Job order
A client company's request for the agency to fill a position. The agency-side name for the same opening.
Submission
A candidate the agency has put forward to a client. Counted per client on the Clients screen.
Placement
A candidate who was actually hired. What the agency is paid on, and the number the whole ATS optimises for.
Pipeline stage
One of five: Applied → Screening → Interview → Offer → Hired. Changed by dragging a card.
Booking
A reserved interview slot. Separate from pipeline stage — see the diagram above.
Slot
A bookable interview window, typically 30 minutes. Generated from the event time frame in admin settings.
Client · subsidiary 客戶 · 集團
A company the agency recruits for. Six are Yang Luck group subsidiaries (badged Group); 19 are external clients.
ARC 居留證
Alien Resident Certificate — the residency document for a foreign national in Taiwan. Issued by NIA 移民署. Expiry is tracked on the Compliance screen.
Work permit 工作許可
Permission to be employed in Taiwan, issued by MOL/WDA 勞動部. Separate from the ARC, with its own expiry.
Visa support
Whether an employer will sponsor a work permit. Often the first thing an applicant checks on a job.
PIPA 個資法
Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act. Drives the per-application consent checkbox and the retention and deletion behaviour.
Moderation
Admin review of a position before it becomes publicly visible. Can be switched off platform-wide.
AI score
A CV-screening score shown on each pipeline card. Simulated in this build — a placeholder, not a live model.

Act 1

The applicant: from stranger to confirmed interview

Fourteen screens, in order. An applicant arrives knowing nothing about the platform and leaves holding a specific 30-minute interview slot with a named company. Every step below is a real screen from the running build.

Applicant · student@yangluck.demo
1.1

Arriving — the company directory

/

The home page is a directory, not a marketing splash. An applicant sees who is actually hiring within the first screenful — company name, industry, a one-line description, and the open positions at each. The goal is to answer "is there anything here for me?" before asking anyone to sign up.

{{IMG:pub-01-home}}

What's on the screen

Company card
One per participating employer. Shows the company name in Chinese and English, an industry tag (製造 Manufacturing, 營造 Construction, 旅宿 Hospitality, 人力派遣 Staffing), and a short description.
Open positions
Up to two live roles listed inside each card, so the applicant can judge fit without navigating away.
View & Book
Opens that company's profile page, where the booking flow starts. The wording is deliberate — booking, not just browsing.
View all
Goes to the full directory at /browse.
Top bar
Home · Companies · Jobs, plus Log In / Sign Up while signed out, and the EN / VN / 中 language switcher.
Why a directory first. The audience is applicants who often haven't heard of the mid-size Taiwanese manufacturers and construction firms doing the hiring. Leading with real company names and industries does more work than a headline would.
1.2

Choosing a role

/get-started

Sign-up forks here. Applicants and employers need different fields, different verification, and different approval rules, so the platform asks which you are before showing a form rather than branching inside one.

{{IMG:pub-02-get-started}}
I'm a Student
Goes to /register. Self-serve — the account is usable as soon as the email is verified.
I'm a Recruiter
Goes to /recruiter/signup. Requires admin approval before the account can post jobs.
1.3

Registering — email verification first

/register

Sign-up opens with just email and password, and sends a verification code before collecting anything else. Nothing is asked twice, and an abandoned sign-up leaves no half-built profile behind.

{{IMG:pub-04-register-student}}
Email
Becomes the account identifier and the address interview notifications are sent to. Normalised to lower-case on submit.
Password
Minimum six characters, with a show/hide toggle.
Send verification code
Emails a code and advances to code entry. The rest of the profile is collected after verification.
Rate limiting. Authentication endpoints are limited on two rings — a generous per-IP cap so a whole campus behind one NAT isn't locked out by each other's attempts, and a tighter per-account cap that is the real brute-force defence.
1.4

Logging in

/login · /forgot-password

One login screen for all four roles — the account's role decides where you land. Errors distinguish "no such account" from "wrong password", because the two need different next actions.

{{IMG:pub-03-login}}
Email · Password
Password field has a show/hide toggle.
Log In
On success sets a session cookie and redirects by role: applicants to the directory, recruiters to /dashboard/interviews, admins to /admin/interviews.
Account not found
Offers to send you to sign-up with the email pre-filled, rather than a dead end.
Forgot password?
Goes to /forgot-password, which emails a reset code.
PIPA notice
A dismissible banner about Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act. Dismissal is remembered in local storage.
next parameter
Protected pages redirect here with the intended destination attached — e.g. /login?next=/feedback — and you land there after signing in.

Password reset

{{IMG_BARE:pub-06-forgot-password}}

Entering the account email sends a reset code. Same code-based mechanism as sign-up verification, so applicants only ever learn one pattern.

1.5

Building the profile — eight tabs

/profile

The profile is what recruiters actually read, so it is split into eight short tabs rather than one long form. A completion meter shows how much is filled in, and Save Changes & Next walks the tabs in order — the form is designed to be finished in sittings, not one go.

{{IMG:app-01-profile-basic}}

Header controls

Photo Upload
Profile picture, shown to recruiters on the candidate card.
Profile completion
A live percentage across all eight tabs. It is a nudge, not a gate — you can apply before reaching 100%.
Save Changes
Saves and stays. Save Changes & Next saves and advances to the next tab.
View Profile
Opens the public card at /applicant/[id] — exactly what a recruiter sees.

The eight tabs

Basic
Full name, phone / WhatsApp, nationality. WhatsApp matters — it is the practical contact channel for Vietnamese and Indonesian applicants.
Education
School, major, study level (大三 Junior, 大四 Senior, 碩一 M1, 碩二 M2). These drive the school and major shown on the recruiter's pipeline card.
Career Preferences
Desired role, location, and industry — what the applicant wants, as opposed to what they've done.
Work Experience
Repeatable entries of employer, role and dates.
Certifications
Licences and language certificates (TOCFL, TOEIC, JLPT), which are often decisive for visa-sponsored roles.
Skills
Tags added one at a time. These appear verbatim as chips on the recruiter's pipeline card — AutoCAD, BIM, SolidWorks, Revit, Excel.
CV / QR
The CV link plus a QR code, so an applicant can show their profile from a phone at an in-person fair.
My Applications
Every application and its current status. Covered at step 13.

1.11

CV and consent

/recruiter/[id] → step booking-form

The last screen before submitting. Identity is pre-filled from the profile; what the applicant must actively confirm is the two things that break downstream — an unreadable CV link, and consent to share their data with this specific employer.

{{IMG:app-15-apply-booking-form}}

Both confirmations checked, ready to submit

{{IMG_BARE:app-16-apply-consent}}
Chosen time
Restated at the top — Saturday, June 6 at 14:00.
Name · Email
Read-only, from the profile.
Position applying for required
The specific role. Applications attach to a position, never to a company alone.
CV Link required
Pre-filled from the profile, editable per application — so an applicant can send a tailored CV to one employer.
Access confirmation required
"I confirm I have set access to 'Anyone with the link can view'." A recruiter who cannot open the CV is the single most common failure in this flow, so it is confirmed by hand.
PIPA consent required
Names the employer and the position, and cites Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act. Consent is per application, not a blanket permission.
Request Booking
Submits. Back returns to the slot picker with the position kept.
Double-booking is blocked. Requesting a slot that clashes with an interview the applicant already holds fails with "You already have a pending or confirmed interview at this time." The guard is on the applicant's own schedule across all employers, not just within one company.

3.6

Offers — what was offered, who authorised it, and the answer

/dashboard/offers

The step between "interviewing well" and "placed" where money is promised. An offer records the exact terms — salary, start date, probation — and cannot be sent until someone with authority approves it, so no candidate is promised terms one recruiter invented.

{{IMG:rec-14-offers}}

The lifecycle

Draft
A recruiter has written terms. Nothing has been promised to anyone.
Approved
Someone holding offer:approve — an admin, account manager, or the client's hiring manager — has signed off on the money.
Sent
The terms are with the candidate.
Accepted / Declined
The answer, with a recorded reason on a decline. An accepted offer is what a placement (step 3.8) is created from, so terms are never retyped.
Drafting and authorising are different capabilities. A recruiter can write an offer but not approve it; a hiring manager approves terms without drafting them. Approving something you cannot see is not a control — which is why approvers also hold read access, and the audit log records both signatures.

3.8

Placements — the guarantee window

/dashboard/placements

A hire is not the end: Taiwanese placement contracts carry a guarantee period, and if the person leaves inside it the fee is exposed to a clawback. This screen tracks every placement from start date through probation and guarantee to completed — or fell-off.

{{IMG:rec-15-placements}}
Lifecycle
placed → started → completed, or fell_off if the candidate leaves inside the guarantee.
Probation vs guarantee
Two different dates on purpose: probation is the employer's right to end the contract; the guarantee is the agency's clawback exposure. They usually differ.
Fee at risk
While a placement is inside its guarantee window, its fee is not yet safely earned — the screen shows how many days remain and what money is still exposed.
Document status
The compliance register's verdict for this person, inline — a placement whose work permit lapses is a problem this row surfaces first.
3.9

Billing — invoices, credit notes, and what is still unbilled

/dashboard/billing

The money side of a placement. Each fee becomes a numbered invoice with 5% 營業稅 computed exactly; payments and corrections are their own recorded documents; and the screen always shows which placements have not been billed yet — the list that is otherwise forgotten.

{{IMG:rec-16-billing}}

The rules the screen enforces

Numbered documents
Invoices are INV-YYYY-NNNN, credit notes CN-YYYY-NNNN — separate sequences, no silent gaps. An accountant reads a gap as a missing document.
One live line per fee
A placement's fee can be billed exactly once — the database itself refuses a duplicate. Voiding an invoice frees the fee to be re-raised on a correction.
Paid is immutable
A paid invoice cannot be voided or edited. The correction is a credit note: its own numbered document both sides can reconcile, with tax at the invoice's rate, so it reverses what was actually charged.
Outstanding, net of credits
The outstanding figure subtracts credit notes — a fully credited invoice is not money anyone is waiting for.
Client fee rates
A client can carry an agreed rate — e.g. 1.2 months of salary, the Taiwan convention. A new placement takes its fee from the rate only when no explicit fee was agreed; a blank fee stays blank rather than looking computed.

Notes & known gaps

Recorded so the manual isn't read as claiming more than the build does.

The AI score is simulated. Every candidate card and the drawer show an AI score, labelled (demo) in the drawer. It is a seeded placeholder for CV screening, not a live model. Treat it as a slot in the design, not a capability.
The slot picker ignores the configured event date. src/components/slot-picker.tsx opens on the build-time constant EVENT_CONFIG.date rather than the event date set in admin settings. Changing the date under Interview Time Frame updates the rest of the app, but the picker still opens on the old day, where an applicant sees "No available slots on this day." Found while capturing this manual; worth fixing before the next live event.
Interview booking and the ATS pipeline are separate systems. Bookings (slots, accept/reject, the schedule) and pipeline stages (Applied → Hired) are deliberately distinct: a candidate can sit in Screening without holding an interview slot, and an interview can be booked without a pipeline card. They meet on the candidate, not in a single status field.
Two recruiter surfaces, one role. Client-company and agency accounts are both recruiter. Clients, Compliance and Reports appear only for the agency account. If an employer reports "missing tabs", this is why.
Captured environment. Every screenshot is from the running application against an isolated demo database — 28 accounts, 25 client companies, 35 positions, 37 candidates, 15 bookings, all fictional. Production data was never used.
