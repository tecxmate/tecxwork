✦ Based on the documentation, you’ve built a highly optimized, single-tenant booking platform tailored for a specific
  event (V-GEN TRIDENT at Taipei Tech). You’ve made smart trade-offs (using Google Drive for storage, atomic locking
  in Neon Postgres, static Next.js deployments) to keep costs near zero.

  To pivot this into a multi-tenant SaaS and build a strong defensive moat, here is the strategic roadmap:

  1. Scaling to a Multi-Tenant SaaS

  Right now, your app is hardcoded for one event. To serve multiple universities or organizations simultaneously, you
  need to transition to a multi-tenant architecture.

   * Database Multi-Tenancy (Row-Level Security): 
      You need an events or organizations table. Every existing table (users, slots, bookings) must get an event_id
  foreign key. You should leverage Postgres Row-Level Security (RLS) in your Neon database so that a query for one
  event physically cannot access data from another event.
   * Dynamic Subdomain Routing:
      Refactor your Next.js App Router to use dynamic tenant routing. Instead of tecxmeet.com/browse, it becomes
  tecxmeet.com/[eventSlug]/browse or [eventSlug].tecxmeet.com. This gives every organizer their own branded URL (e.g.,
  vgen.tecxmeet.com).
   * Role Hierarchy:
      Expand your simple JWT auth. You need:
       * Platform Admin (You: can create events and bill organizers).
       * Event Admin (The University/VSATW: manages their specific event, domains, and recruiters).
       * Recruiters & Students (Scoped only to the events they are registered for).
   * Self-Serve Onboarding & Billing:
      Integrate Stripe. Allow an organizer to visit your site, pay $299 (or whatever your pricing is), and
  automatically spin up an isolated event instance with their logo, custom allowed recruiter domains, and custom
  dates.

  2. Building a Defensive Moat (Hard to Copy)

  Anyone can clone a calendar booking app. To make your micro-SaaS immune to copycats, you need to build value that
  compounds and is heavily integrated into the physical workflow of a career fair.

  A. The "Talent Passport" (Network Effect Moat)
  Right now, students recreate their profile for this one event. Change that. Make the student profile persistent
  across all events hosted on your platform. 
   * If a student attends the NTU fair in May and the Taipei Tech fair in June, their CV, skills, and verified major
     travel with them. 
   * The Moat: Once you have 10,000 Taiwanese students on the platform, organizers will want to use your software
     because the students are already there and don't need to sign up again.

  B. Automated PIPA Compliance (Deep Tech/Legal Moat)
  Your current Google Drive workaround (asking students to share their link manually with a recruiter's email) is
  clever but prone to human error.
   * The Moat: Integrate the Google Drive API. Have the student authenticate with Google once. When a student books a
     slot or scans a QR code, your backend automatically calls the Google API to grant view access to the recruiter's
     email, and automatically revokes it 30 days after the event. 
   * Why it's defensible: Competitors will just build file uploads. By doing programmatic permission management, you
     guarantee absolute PDPA/PIPA compliance for the university, with zero manual work for the student. Universities
     will buy your software simply for the liability protection.

  C. The Physical-Digital Bridge (Hardware/UX Moat)
  You mentioned the QR code system in your notes (students showing a QR code to recruiters at physical booths). Double
  down on this.
   * Pure software companies fail at live events because they don't understand the physical chaos of a career fair.
   * Build features specifically for the physical booth: Recruiter "Walk-up" modes, instant iPad check-ins, or live
     maps of the venue showing which booths currently have empty interview slots. If your software makes the physical
     event run smoother, it's impossible for a purely digital clone to compete.

  D. ATS Integrations (Enterprise Moat)
  Recruiters hate downloading CSVs.
   * Integrate with major Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) like Workday, Greenhouse, or Lever. 
   * When a student books an interview, use an API to push their profile directly into the recruiter's company ATS as
     a "New Candidate." If you save recruiters from manual data entry, they will demand that universities use your
     platform.

  Next Steps:
  If you want to start this transition, the immediate technical step is refactoring your Neon database schema in
  src/lib/db/schema.ts to include an events table and link your users and bookings to it. Would you like to start
  planning that schema migration?

✦ You hit the nail on the head. By relying on Google Drive links, you are intentionally blinding your platform to the
  actual value of the network: the candidate data. 

  If you want to build a highly defensive SaaS that organizers and recruiters will pay for, you need to transition
  from being a "dumb scheduling pipe" to a "smart talent database."

  Building a structured profile system (like CakeResume) is the ultimate moat. However, it introduces new challenges.
  Here is a breakdown of the trade-offs and the best strategy to execute this without killing your conversion rate.

  The Value of Structured Data (Why you should do it)

   1. The "Talent Passport" Moat: If you hold the structured data (Work History, Education, GPA, Projects), students
      only need to fill it out once. When the next career fair happens, they just click "Apply with V-GEN." This
      creates a lock-in effect.
   2. Search & Discovery for Recruiters: Right now, recruiters can only search by the basic skills array. If you have
      structured data, you can sell a premium feature to recruiters: "Filter all 1,000 attendees to find Junior CS
      students with React experience who graduate in 2027."
   3. ATS Integrations: As mentioned before, you cannot push a Google Drive link into an enterprise ATS like Workday.
      You need structured JSON data (Name, Experience, Education) to automatically create candidate profiles for
      recruiters.
   4. Standardized PDFs: Generating a unified, clean, beautifully branded PDF for every student makes the recruiters'
      lives 10x easier. They don't have to read 100 different, badly formatted Word documents.

  The Danger: The "Blank Canvas" Drop-off

  The biggest risk of the "CakeResume" approach is friction.
  Currently, a student signs up in 30 seconds by pasting a Drive link. If you force them to manually type out their
  entire work history, education, and bullet points on a mobile phone, 50% of them will give up and close the tab.

  The Winning Strategy: Progressive Profiling & AI Parsing

  You must collect the data, but you must make it effortless for the student. Here is how you approach it:

  Step 1: Replace Google Drive with Direct PDF Uploads (Short Term)
  Instead of a Drive link, give them a simple file upload button. You can use Cloudflare R2 or AWS S3 (very cheap).
   * Why? It removes the "Permission Denied" headache of Google Drive immediately. Your app securely hosts the PDF and
     generates temporary, secure viewing links for the recruiters.

  Step 2: Implement "Resume Parsing" (The Magic Trick)
  Do not make them type everything manually. Let them upload their existing PDF CV, and use an API (like OpenAI's API
  or a dedicated parser like Affinda) to automatically read the PDF and extract the structured data into your
  database.
   * The UX: Student uploads my_resume.pdf ➔ Loading spinner for 3 seconds ➔ Your app says "We found 2 degrees and 3
     work experiences. Look good?" ➔ The student just clicks "Confirm."
   * Result: You get the deep, structured data, but the student experiences zero friction.

  Step 3: The "V-GEN Standardized CV"
  Once the data is in your Neon database, you can use a library like @react-pdf/renderer to generate a beautiful,
  standardized PDF. When a recruiter clicks a student's profile, they don't see the student's original ugly PDF; they
  see your beautifully branded, highly legible "V-GEN CV."

  How this affects your database schema

  To support this, your applicant_profiles table would eventually need related tables. Instead of just skills: text[],

  Summary Recommendation

  Do not build a manual form-builder first. 
  Start by changing the Google Drive link to a direct PDF file upload using Cloudflare R2 (or Vercel Blob). This fixes
  your immediate permission issues and secures the files inside your system. 

  Then, in V2, integrate an AI parser to read those uploaded PDFs and secretly build your structured "Talent Database"
  in the background. That database is what you will eventually sell to recruiters and organizers.
